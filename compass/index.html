Compass · practical AI in accountable operations

How I bring AI into an operation I am accountable for

Compass is a private internal application for property administration in my own UK business. This public case study uses synthetic examples to demonstrate transferable operational workflows. The application is under development and testing; it is not a commercial SaaS product or an external client deployment.

My contribution: I defined the operating requirements, workflow logic, decision rights and controls, and implemented the application using AI-assisted development.

The simple operating model

  1. Evidence

    Authoritative operational data, documents and events

  3. AI proposes

    Interprets bounded evidence and suggests action

  5. Human decides

    Judgement, ownership and approval remain human

  7. Governed action

    Only approved actions reach existing systems

Authoritative facts, deterministic business rules, AI interpretation and human decision rights remain deliberately distinct.

A governance decision I had to make

When Compass could not truthfully record the origin of an AI-proposed action, it blocked the action rather than weaken the audit trail.

Governance should be capable of saying no even when the technology can say yes.

What this demonstrates about my approach

  • Translate operating models into implementation

    Move from an operating question to working controls, workflows and evidence rather than stopping at a concept.

  • Design decision rights and governance

    Separate what AI may propose from what policy permits, what people approve and what systems execute.

  • Apply practical AI judgement

    Use AI where it adds interpretive leverage without allowing it to become the source of truth or decision authority.

  • Make operations observable

    Use recorded evidence to show how work moves, where humans intervene and where escalation occurs.

These capabilities extend the strategy-to-execution discipline from my senior Strategy and Operations career. Compass is supporting evidence of my approach; it is not a claim of enterprise-scale deployment or financial ROI.

Case study

The examples below use synthetic data. The workflows, control boundaries and decision logic shown are implemented in the private application.

From operational exception to accountable action

A detected exception does not become an AI decision. Compass separates authoritative evidence, deterministic rules, proposal, approval and execution so accountability remains explicit.

  1. Exception identified

    Material adverse variance

    A deterministic rule identifies an exception from authoritative evidence.

  2. Response proposed

    Create a management action

    Policy validation: valid

    AI proposes a bounded response using the available evidence.

  3. Human decision

    Owner: Operations Lead

    Priority: High

    Due date: 07 Oct 2026

    Ownership, priority and timing remain explicit human decisions.

  4. Controlled execution

    1 step approved

    Approval does not change the authoritative record. Execution is a separate action through an existing governed service.

The model proposes what could be done. Policy determines what is permitted. A person determines what will be done.

Implementation note

I defined the operating requirements, workflow logic, decision rights and controls, and implemented Compass using AI-assisted development. The repository includes automated testing, validation and evaluation around the governed workflows; business actions and release decisions remain human-controlled.

Financial calculations stay deterministic and outside the language model; AI can interpret bounded evidence or propose action, but it does not calculate or overwrite authoritative financial facts.
